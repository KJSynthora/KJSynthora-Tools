Frequently Asked Questions

Common GST questions answered

How is GST calculated in India? ▼
To add GST: GST Amount = Base Price × GST Rate ÷ 100. Total = Base Price + GST. Example: ₹1000 base at 18% = ₹180 GST = ₹1180 total.
To extract GST (reverse): Base = Total ÷ (1 + Rate/100). Example: ₹1180 ÷ 1.18 = ₹1000 base. GST = ₹1180 − ₹1000 = ₹180.
What is CGST and SGST? ▼
CGST (Central GST) goes to the Central Government. SGST (State GST) goes to the State Government. For intra-state sales, the total GST is split equally: a 18% GST means 9% CGST + 9% SGST. For inter-state sales, IGST (full 18%) applies and goes to the Centre, which then distributes to states.
What items are exempt from GST (0% slab)? ▼
Exempt items include: fresh fruits and vegetables, milk, eggs, unprocessed cereals, jaggery, educational services, healthcare services, public transport (bus/rail), and religious services. These are either 0% or completely outside GST scope.
What is the GST rate on services? ▼
Most services attract 18% GST — including IT services, consulting, banking, insurance, and professional services. However, healthcare (0%), educational services (0%), and some government services are exempt. Restaurants attract 5% (without ITC) or 18% (with ITC for AC restaurants).
Who is required to register for GST? ▼
Mandatory GST registration is required if: annual turnover exceeds ₹40 lakh (goods) or ₹20 lakh (services), you supply goods/services inter-state, you are an e-commerce operator, or you make taxable supplies as an agent or through e-commerce platforms.
What is Input Tax Credit (ITC)? ▼
ITC allows businesses to deduct the GST paid on purchases (inputs) from the GST payable on sales (output). For example, if you paid ₹1800 GST on raw materials and collected ₹3600 GST on sales, you only pay ₹1800 net to the government. This avoids cascading taxation.
What is composition scheme under GST? ▼
Small businesses with turnover below ₹1.5 crore (₹75 lakh for services) can opt for the Composition Scheme. They pay a fixed flat rate (1–5% depending on business type) instead of regular GST rates. However, they cannot claim ITC or issue tax invoices.
