Frequently Asked Questions

Why can't I open WebP images on some devices or apps? +
WebP is a modern format created by Google in 2010. While most modern browsers support it, older software like Windows Photo Viewer (pre-2019), some mobile gallery apps, certain design tools like older versions of Photoshop, and many CMS platforms still don't support WebP natively. Converting to PNG or JPG ensures universal compatibility across all devices, apps, and platforms.
Will I lose quality converting WebP to PNG? +
If the source WebP is lossless, converting to PNG preserves 100% quality since PNG is also lossless. If the source WebP was saved with lossy compression, the visual quality at that compression level is preserved in PNG (which doesn't add further loss). PNG files will be larger because they don't use lossy compression. For photos, JPG output at 85–95% quality gives a good balance of size and quality.
Does WebP support transparency? +
Yes, WebP supports alpha channel transparency (like PNG), unlike JPG which doesn't support transparency at all. When converting transparent WebP to PNG, the alpha channel is preserved perfectly. When converting to JPG (which has no alpha support), our tool lets you choose a background color to fill the transparent areas — white by default.
How many files can I convert at once? +
You can batch convert up to 50 images at once. For larger batches, convert in multiple sessions. All files are processed sequentially in your browser using the Canvas API, so conversion speed depends on your device's CPU and the resolution of the images. High-resolution images (4K+) may take a few seconds each.
Is the conversion 100% private? +
Completely. Our tool uses the browser's built-in Canvas API to read and convert images. No image data is ever sent to any server — you can verify this yourself in your browser's DevTools Network tab during conversion. You'll see zero network requests. The converted images are generated entirely in your browser's memory and downloaded directly to your device.
What's the difference between PNG and JPG for converted files? +
PNG uses lossless compression — no quality loss, but larger files. Best for: screenshots, logos, graphics with text, images with transparency. JPG uses lossy compression — smaller files with slight quality loss (invisible at 85%+). Best for: photographs, web images where file size matters. WebP was designed to replace both — it's typically 25–35% smaller than comparable PNG or JPG at the same quality.
