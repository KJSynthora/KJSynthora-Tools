Currency Exchange — Guides & Money Transfer Tips

Everything you need to know about international money transfers, exchange rates, and how to send money cheaply across borders.

📖 Complete Guide

How to Get the Best Exchange Rate When Sending Money Abroad

Most people lose 3-5% on every international transfer without realizing it. Here's how to minimize fees and maximize the amount received on the other end.

Understand the mid-market rate: This is the "real" exchange rate — the midpoint between buy and sell prices in the global forex market. Banks add a markup of 1.5-3% on top. Services like Wise and Revolut charge much lower markups (0.3-0.6%).

Compare total cost, not just the rate: A service offering a great rate might charge high flat fees. For small transfers, flat fees matter more. For large transfers, the rate percentage matters more. Use the Provider Comparison table in this tool to see exact amounts for your transfer.

Timing matters: Currency markets move 24/7. Rates can vary by 0.5-2% throughout the day during high-volatility periods. The London-New York overlap (2:30-6:30 PM IST) typically has the most liquidity and tightest spreads.

Avoid airport and hotel exchanges: These can charge markups of 5-15%. ATM withdrawals abroad are usually better, but check your bank's foreign transaction fees first.

🏦 Comparison

Wise vs Revolut vs Bank Transfer — Which Is Actually Cheapest?

A detailed breakdown of the most popular money transfer services, their real costs, and when each one makes sense for different transfer amounts and corridors.

Wise (formerly TransferWise): Charges the mid-market rate + a small percentage fee (0.4-0.6% typically). For most corridors (USD→INR, GBP→EUR), Wise is consistently in the top 2 cheapest. Best for: regular international transfers, freelancers receiving payment abroad.

Revolut: Free for small amounts during weekdays (up to a monthly limit depending on your plan). Charges 1% on weekends and after limits. Premium and Metal plans have higher free limits. Best for: frequent small transfers, travelers who already use Revolut for spending.

Traditional bank transfer (SWIFT): Charges 1.5-3% markup plus flat fees ($15-50 per transfer). Slow (1-3 business days). Best for: when the recipient only has a bank account and no app-based alternatives, or for countries with limited Wise coverage.

PayPal: Charges 2.5-3.5% plus currency conversion fees. Best avoided for large transfers. Only use when the recipient specifically needs PayPal (freelancers, some businesses).

🇮🇳 India Specific

USD to INR: What Drives the Rupee's Exchange Rate?

The INR is Asia's most actively traded emerging market currency. Understanding what moves the rupee helps you time your transfers better and predict short-term rate direction.

RBI intervention: The Reserve Bank of India actively manages the rupee's value through forex market interventions. When the rupee weakens too fast, the RBI sells dollars from reserves to support it. This creates a relatively stable rate environment compared to free-floating currencies.

Oil prices: India imports ~85% of its crude oil needs. When oil prices rise, India needs more dollars to pay for imports, weakening the rupee. Oil price spikes and INR weakness are closely correlated.

FII flows: Foreign Institutional Investors bring dollars into Indian stock and bond markets. When FIIs buy Indian assets, it strengthens the rupee. When they sell (risk-off periods), the rupee weakens.

US Dollar Index (DXY): The INR tracks the DXY closely. When the Fed raises rates, dollars strengthen globally, weakening all emerging market currencies including the rupee. This is why US Fed decisions impact your USD/INR rate directly.

💡 Money Tips

NRI Guide: The Cheapest Ways to Send Money to India in 2026

For NRIs sending remittances to India, choosing the right service can save thousands of rupees per transfer. Here's the complete comparison for 2026.

Wise (recommended for most): Sends directly to Indian bank accounts (NRE, NRO, savings). Typically offers rates 1.5-2% better than banks. For $1,000, you save ₹1,200-1,600 compared to a bank transfer.

Google Pay (US→India): Powered by Western Union, with competitive rates for smaller amounts. Instant transfers to linked Indian bank accounts. Free for transfers under $500 per transaction.

Remitly: Good for large transfers (>$10,000) with locked-in rates and often promotional rates for first-time users. Offers Express (minutes) and Economy (bank rate, 3-5 days) options.

Bank-to-bank SWIFT: Most expensive but necessary when the recipient doesn't have a smartphone or you need a paper trail for large amounts. Add ₹3,000-5,000 in costs per transfer vs. Wise.

Crypto (USDT → INR): Lowest fees (0.1-0.2%) but requires both sender and receiver to have crypto exchange accounts. Rate risk during transfer window is a consideration. Best for tech-savvy users making large transfers.

📊 Forex Basics

Understanding Forex: Spot Rate, Forward Rate & Currency Pairs Explained

From USD/INR to EUR/GBP — forex terminology can be confusing. Here's a plain-English guide to understanding how currency markets actually work.

Currency pair notation: USD/INR = 83.5 means 1 USD buys 83.5 INR. The first currency is the base (what you're buying/selling). The second is the quote (what you're measuring in). When the number goes up, the base currency strengthened.

Spot rate vs forward rate: Spot rate is the current price for immediate settlement (what this tool shows). Forward rate is agreed now but settled in the future — used by businesses to hedge currency risk for future transactions.

Bid-ask spread: Banks and brokers quote two rates — the bid (what they'll buy from you) and the ask (what they'll sell to you). The difference is their profit. Wise and Revolut have very tight spreads. Banks have wide spreads (their main profit source on forex).

Cross-currency rates: When converting SGD to INR directly, the provider calculates SGD→USD→INR behind the scenes. This "cross" calculation can add extra spread costs, making it worth checking if direct conversion is available.

🌍 Travel Tips

Currency Exchange for International Travel: Airport vs ATM vs Card

Heading abroad? The wrong currency choice can cost you 8-15% of your travel budget. Here's the definitive guide to spending money overseas cheaply.

Never exchange at airports: Airport exchange booths charge 8-15% over the mid-market rate. If you must, exchange only a small amount for immediate taxi/food needs and convert more at a city bank or ATM.

ATM withdrawal strategy: Using your home debit card at a foreign ATM typically gives close to the mid-market rate. Your bank may charge a foreign transaction fee (1-3%) and the ATM may charge a flat fee ($3-7). For amounts over $200, this is usually still cheaper than airport exchange.

Multi-currency travel cards: Wise, Revolut, and Niyo Global (for Indians) let you hold multiple currencies at near-market rates. Load INR, convert to USD/EUR/GBP at good rates before traveling. Spend with the card abroad — no foreign transaction fees.

Credit cards with zero forex fees: Some credit cards (like HDFC Regalia, Axis Atlas) charge zero forex markup and often offer reward points on international spending. Pair with travel insurance for the best travel finance setup.
